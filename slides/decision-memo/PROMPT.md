Make a three-page presentation for the operations lead at Cedar Support to help
decide whether to extend a trial of suggested replies. Explain the trade-off
between handling time and quality, what the evidence supports, and what the
team should learn next.

---

Cedar Support is a fictional customer-support team. These figures are synthetic,
hand-authored examples rather than results of an actual trial.

Two groups each handled 120 tickets during one week. Assignment was not
randomized, and ticket difficulty and agent experience were not controlled.

- Standard workflow: median handling time of 12 minutes; 108 tickets passed
  quality review; 9 reopened within seven days of closure.
- Suggested replies: median handling time of 9 minutes; 102 tickets passed
  quality review; 15 reopened within seven days of closure.

Quality review and reopening are separate measures: a ticket can pass review
and later reopen. Each rate uses all 120 tickets in its group. The quality rates
are 90% and 85%; the reopen rates are 7.5% and 12.5%.

We have no raw timing observations, variance estimates, confidence intervals,
labor costs or customer-satisfaction results. The groups cannot establish
causality, and their median times cannot tell us total hours saved.

The proposed next step is a longer randomized trial with comparable ticket
categories and human review retained. Quality and reopen-rate acceptance
criteria would be agreed before starting; no numerical thresholds have been set.
