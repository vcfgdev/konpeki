# One-pager or card

Use for a bounded brief, summary, reference sheet, invitation, or information
card. Apply the [shared guidance](../SKILL.md#before-authoring) and the
[authoring floor](../floor.md). A résumé or presentation slide uses its own guide
even when it contains only one page.

- Deliver one explicit page. Use the `a4` preset for an unspecified printable
  one-pager; for a card, choose dimensions for its destination. Landscape, portrait,
  square, and custom sizes are all valid; do not force cards into a slide ratio.
- Establish the page's reader task: understand a proposal, compare options,
  recall information, or act on an invitation. Let that task determine emphasis
  and section order instead of imposing a fixed number of content blocks.
- Retain the evidence needed to interpret the main message. A headline, a
  prominent number, and a call to action are not sufficient if scope, units,
  conditions, or supporting facts have disappeared.
- Use columns for genuinely parallel or scannable material. Use flowing prose
  for an argument. Do not turn every paragraph into an equally prominent card.
- Add contact details or a next action only when the brief calls for them.
  Keep them findable without giving boilerplate the same weight as the content.
- Treat the one-page limit as an editorial constraint, not permission to use
  tiny type. Condense repetition and recompose; if required content still cannot
  fit legibly, surface that conflict rather than adding a second page silently.

Check the whole page at its intended print or display size, including small cards.
Whitespace should reveal hierarchy, not leave essential copy crowded into one
corner while a decorative region consumes the page.
